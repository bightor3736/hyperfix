# Hyperfix — Market Research

*Prepared 2026-06-07. Honest internal doc for the founder. Figures cited from public 2024–2026 market reports and reviews; where I'm interpolating or estimating, it's marked **(est.)**. Market-sizing reports in this space are notoriously generous — treat the dollar figures as directional, not gospel.*

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **The market is real and growing fast.** ADHD-app market sizing lands around **$1.9–2.8B in 2024–2025** depending on the report, with **~15–18% CAGR** projected to 2033–2036. Tailwind is genuine: adult diagnoses up sharply, TikTok awareness enormous (#ADHD ~3.9M TikTok posts, billions of views).
- **The audience is the best and worst customer you can have.** Loud, community-driven, evangelical recommenders — *and* high-churn, novelty-seeking, and prone to abandoning the thing that was supposed to help them. Your retention curve is the whole game.
- **"Tracking hyperfixations" is almost certainly a feature, not a company.** A neutral logger is a vitamin. Nobody wakes up in distress because their hyperfixation went unlogged. The pain that gets people to pay is *task initiation* ("I can't start"), *shame* ("I feel broken"), and *focus*.
- **Gamification alone is a known trap.** Gamified habit apps hit a ~3-week novelty cliff (one cited study: ~67% abandonment by week 4 for gamified vs ~38% non-gamified). The apps that survive (Forest, Finch) wrap the game around a *real task* or a *real emotion*, not around points for their own sake.
- **Strongest bet: the painkiller wedge.** Use the hyperfixation as *bait* to start the boring task, with **proof-of-action focus timer as the hero**. The fun thing is the carrot; getting the user moving is the value. The identity/social card is the growth loop on top, not the core.

## 2. The Market

**Size & growth.** Reports cluster around:

- ADHD apps market: **~$1.91B (2024) → ~$4.06B (2029)** in one report; another puts **$2.78B (2025)**; a third **$2.4B (2024) → $7.7B (2033) at ~15.6% CAGR**.
- ADHD digital therapy / DTx: **~18% CAGR** through the mid-2030s.

The spread tells you these are top-down analyst guesses, not bottoms-up truth. The *signal* is consistent: double-digit growth, lots of new entrants, capital flowing into neurodiversity tooling.

**Why it's growing.**

- **Diagnoses up, especially adults.** US adult stimulant prescriptions (ages 20–39) rose ~30% 2020–2022; non-stimulant +81%; new diagnoses remained ~31% above pre-pandemic levels in 2023. ~11.4% of US children carry an ADHD diagnosis.
- **TikTok awareness (a double-edged sword).** Half a billion+ views on top #ADHD content; millions of posts. *But* studies find <50% of symptom claims in popular videos match DSM criteria, with ~27% outright misleading. This drives a huge wave of **self-identifying** ADHD users who want tools and language — a marketing goldmine and a credibility minefield.

**Audience characteristics — read this twice:**

- **Evangelical.** They recommend tools constantly, in communities, in comments, in "what's in my ADHD toolkit" videos. Word-of-mouth and shareability matter more here than in almost any consumer category.
- **Identity-forward.** ADHD is worn as identity, not just diagnosis. They buy products that *say something about them*.
- **Novelty-seeking + high churn.** The same dopamine wiring that makes them try your app makes them drop it for the next shiny thing. New-app honeymoons are short.
- **Shame-loaded.** Most have a history of "productivity tools that made me feel worse." Anything that punishes (dead streaks, guilt, red numbers) gets uninstalled *and resented*.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

**Read of the board:** The durable players each own *one painful job* and wrap any gamification around a *real action or real feeling*. The purely gamified player (Habitica) is precisely the one with the documented retention problem. Hyperfix is currently described as *closest to Habitica's model* — that should worry you.

## 4. The Gamification Retention Problem

**The cliff is documented.** Gamified apps show higher engagement in weeks 1–2 (one cited figure: +41%) but **higher abandonment by week 4** (~67% vs ~38% for non-gamified, per a cited Stanford Persuasive Tech figure — treat as illustrative). The pattern is consistent across fitness and habit apps: novelty 3–7 days, fade by week 2, ghost-town by week 4–6. Habitica is the archetype — "week one amazing, week six your character is dead and you haven't opened it in ten days."

**Why Forest and Finch survive it:**

- **Forest** wraps the game around a *real task* (don't touch your phone) and adds *real-world stakes* (actual trees planted). When the points stop feeling new, the focus benefit and the real tree remain.
- **Finch** wraps the game around a *real emotion* (self-compassion, anxiety relief). The pet creates genuine attachment; the value is the feeling, not the score.

**The principle:** **Points decay. Tasks and emotions don't.** Gamification is a fine *accelerant* and a terrible *engine*.

**Implication for Hyperfix — direct:** XP for "logging your hyperfixation" is points-for-points. That is exactly the structure that hits the 3-week cliff. The mechanics most likely to survive are the ones tied to a real outcome: **the proof-of-action focus timer** (Forest-like: did real work) and **streak freezes / forgiving streaks** (Finch-like: anti-shame, reduces the punishment that causes rage-quits). Those are your retention assets. The 7-tier vanity ladder and the logging XP are the *decorations*. Don't confuse them.

## 5. Where Hyperfix Fits — Painkiller vs. Vitamin

**Be blunt: "tracking hyperfixations" is a vitamin.** It's pleasant, identity-affirming, and zero-urgency. Nobody is in pain because their hyperfixation went untracked. A neutral hyperfixation logger is **a feature, not a company** — easily cloned, easily abandoned, and it competes with the user's Notes app and "for free."

The **painkillers** this audience actually pays for:

1. **"I physically cannot start the boring task."** (Task initiation — the #1 ADHD pain.)
2. **"I feel broken / every productivity tool has shamed me."** (Anti-shame, self-compassion.)
3. **"I want to do the fun thing without guilt."** (Permission + structure for the hyperfixation itself.)

The good news: **Hyperfix already touches all three.** The hyperfixation is the high-dopamine fun thing (#3). The proof-of-action timer is task initiation (#1). Forgiving streaks/freezes are anti-shame (#2). The strategic error would be to make the *logger* the hero. The logger is the hook; the **painkiller has to be the product.**

The framing that turns a feature into a company: **"Hyperfix uses the thing you're obsessed with to trick your brain into doing the thing you've been avoiding."** That is a painkiller with a built-in viral hook.

## 6. Demand Signals

**What ADHD users actually search for and ask for (high intent):**

- "Can't start tasks" / "task paralysis" / "ADHD task initiation" — huge, persistent.
- "Body doubling" — now mainstream; a 2025 VR study showed faster completion with a human *or AI* double; accountability check-ins cited as lifting goal achievement from ~25% to ~95%.
- "Dopamine menu" — trending framework (starter / side / dessert) for self-administering motivation.
- "ADHD focus timer," "how to focus with ADHD."
- Anti-shame language: "ADHD isn't laziness," "I'm not broken."

**What nobody searches for:**

- **"ADHD game"** — essentially zero intent. People don't want a game; they want to *function*. Gamification is a delivery mechanism you sneak in, not a value prop you lead with.
- **"Hyperfixation tracker"** — near-zero search volume. The *word* "hyperfixation" is beloved in-community (great for brand voice and shareables), but it's not a thing people go looking to *track*. It's identity vocabulary, not a job-to-be-done.

**Takeaway:** Lead acquisition with the searched painkillers (focus, can't-start, body doubling, anti-shame). Use "hyperfixation" as **brand/identity flavor and shareable-content fuel**, not as the functional pitch.

## 7. Three Strategic Positioning Bets

### Bet A — Painkiller wedge: hyperfixation as *bait* to start the boring task *(recommended)*

**Position:** "Channel your hyperfixation to actually get stuff done." **Proof-of-action focus timer is the hero.** The hyperfixation is the carrot/reward; the value delivered is task initiation and completed focus sessions.

- **Pros:** Attacks the #1 paid pain (can't-start). Aligns with what retains (Forest model: game wrapped around real action). Maps to high-intent search terms. The hyperfixation angle is a genuine, novel *differentiator* on a crowded focus-timer shelf.
- **Cons:** Harder to build than a logger. Puts you in direct combat with Forest/Focusmate/Tiimo. "Proof of action" must be lightweight or it becomes friction (the opposite of helpful for ADHD).

### Bet B — Anti-shame / expressive-identity social app (Finch + BeReal energy)

**Position:** "The app that celebrates your beautiful chaotic brain." Shareable profile cards, tier identity, community, zero judgment.

- **Pros:** Plays to the audience's strongest traits (evangelical, identity-forward, shareable). The neo-brutalist aesthetic + shareable cards is a *growth loop* if it hits. Anti-shame is a real painkiller (#2).
- **Cons:** Social apps live or die on network density — brutal cold-start. Identity/expression is a *vitamin* unless paired with utility. Monetizing identity at $39/yr is hard; identity apps usually monetize via cosmetics/freemium, not subscriptions.

### Bet C — Neutral hyperfixation logger *(weakest — do not lead here)*

**Position:** "Track what you're obsessed with."

- **Pros:** Cheapest to build; clean brand fit.
- **Cons:** Vitamin. No urgency, no defensibility, no search demand, hits the gamification cliff, competes with free Notes. This is a feature inside A or B, never the company.

**Recommendation: Bet A as the core, with B as the growth layer.** Ship the painkiller (hyperfixation → focus session → proof of action), keep forgiving streaks/freezes as the anti-shame retention spine, and bolt on shareable identity cards as the viral loop. The hyperfixation concept earns its place as *bait and brand*, not as the product's job. Reserve "Power-Up" ($39/yr) for things that deepen the painkiller (advanced timers, smart task-bridging, body-doubling-style accountability) rather than cosmetic tiers — people pay to function, not to decorate.

## 8. Key Risks

1. **The 3-week cliff.** If logging XP and the tier ladder are the engine, you will replicate Habitica's churn. Mitigation: make the *focus timer + forgiving streaks* the engine.
2. **Vitamin trap.** If "track your hyperfixation" stays the headline, you have a feature competing with free Notes. Mitigation: lead with task initiation.
3. **Audience churn is structural.** Even a great app fights a novelty-seeking base. Plan for re-engagement, not just engagement.
4. **Shame backfire.** Streaks, levels, and "you broke your streak" can punish exactly the people you're serving. Forgiving streaks must be aggressively forgiving; tiers must never feel like failure.
5. **Credibility / self-diagnosis wave.** Half the inbound audience is self-identified off questionable TikTok content. Great for TAM, risky for tone — avoid clinical claims you can't back.
6. **Crowded focus-timer shelf.** Forest, Tiimo, Llama Life, Focusmate all own pieces. Differentiation must be sharp.
7. **Monetization mismatch.** $39/yr is reasonable, but only if the paid tier gates a *painkiller*, not cosmetics. ADHD users abandon subscriptions fast; annual billing helps but trial-to-paid will be your hardest metric.
8. **Web-app-only friction.** Competitors are native mobile with notifications, widgets, and lock-screen presence — core to ADHD habit formation. A web app may struggle to be *present* at the moment of need. Validate whether PWA/notifications are enough.

## Open Questions to Validate (user-interview script)

Run 15–20 interviews with diagnosed *and* self-identified ADHD adults before building further. Ask:

1. Walk me through the last time you couldn't start a task you needed to do. What did you actually do?
2. What apps/tools are in your current "ADHD toolkit," and which have you abandoned — and why did you drop them?
3. When you're deep in a hyperfixation, how do you feel about it — proud, guilty, both? Would you *want* to log it? Why?
4. Have you ever used your hyperfixation to get yourself to do something boring? How did that work?
5. Tell me about a productivity app that made you feel *worse*. What did it do?
6. How do you feel when an app tells you you broke your streak?
7. Have you tried body doubling or a dopamine menu? Did they help? Would you pay for them?
8. Would you share a "profile card" about your brain/obsessions publicly? With whom, and why or why not?
9. What's the most you've paid for an ADHD/productivity app, and what made it worth it (or not)?
10. If a focus timer required "proof" you did real work to earn a reward, would that feel motivating or like surveillance/friction?
11. *(Pricing/positioning probe)* Which sentence makes you want to download it: "Track your hyperfixations" or "Use your obsession to start the thing you've been avoiding"?

**Decision gate:** If interviewees light up at task-initiation and the "obsession-as-bait" framing — but shrug at "tracking hyperfixations" — that confirms Bet A. If they instead light up at sharing cards and community and shrug at the timer, you have a Bet B (social) company and a very different roadmap. Find out which *before* you build the tier ladder.
